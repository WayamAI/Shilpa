# Shilpa climbs from parts to products

Shilpa is already built the way the strongest 2026 systems are built: an LLM writes a typed, checkable spec, an exact kernel runs it, and independent checks verify the result. Getting from flanges to "design an entire bicycle" does not need a new paradigm. It needs four things. The first is a **spec layer above the part IR**: product tree, skeleton, ports and mates. The second is a **much larger part vocabulary**: sweep, loft, shell, helix, sheet metal, tube weldments and standard-part factories. The third is a **deterministic assembly solver with interference, clearance and DOF checks**. The fourth is a **test-driven verification harness** that grades whether requirements are met, not just whether the model builds. The field's numbers show why this matters. Fine-tuned models reach about 90% IoU and near-zero invalid rates on sketch-and-extrude datasets. The same frontier model Shilpa uses (GPT-5.5) collapses on real industrial parts, with 0.565–0.812 executability and at most 0.54 Solid IoU, and scores only 52% on assemblable designs ([RealCADBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03773); [MUSE](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28579v2)). On the commercial side, nobody ships fully mated assemblies from text, sheet-metal generation or validated manufacturability. Leo AI's assemblies arrive unmated, and Zoo's assembly mates are still on its roadmap ([Engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/leo-ai-can-now-generate-full-cad-assemblies/); [Zoo](https://zoo.dev/blog/zoo-design-studio-v1)). A template-driven bicycle with real mates and checks is feasible engineering, not research. Open-ended "any product", freeform NURBS from text and closed-loop linkages are still research-stage. Shilpa's 97% build accuracy is roughly an executability score; it does not show readiness for complex products. Readiness should be measured on a ladder (R1–R5) anchored to CADTestBench, Text2CAD-Bench, CADEngBench, MUSE and RealCADBench, with internal suites for the assembly and whole-product levels that no public benchmark covers.

## Code-generating agents won, then hit the industrial wall

Three lineages have competed. **Sequence and B-rep generators** come first. DeepCAD started them; BrepGen, DTGBrepGen, BrepDiff, HoLa and BrepGPT followed in 2024–2025, and TG-Diff and BrepForge arrived in 2026. They can produce curved, freeform solids that code methods cannot. However, they are mostly unconditional or conditioned on class or point cloud, and they output no editable feature history. BrepGPT's "text conditioning" is a chain: GPT-4o makes an image, the image becomes a point cloud, and the point cloud conditions the B-rep model ([BrepGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15563); [BrepGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22171)). None of these offers dimension-accurate control from text, so none fits an engineering agent's core path.

The second lineage, **LLMs and VLMs that write CAD code** (overwhelmingly CadQuery), took over in 2025. Reinforcement learning against a geometric reward turned out to matter most. CAD-Coder cut its invalid rate from 5.3% (SFT only) to 1.45% using GRPO with a Chamfer reward. On the same test set, zero-shot GPT-4o produced invalid code 93% of the time and Claude 3.7 Sonnet 47% of the time ([CAD-Coder](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19713)). cadrille, a 2B Qwen2-VL model trained with online RL, reached **90.2 IoU with 0.0% invalid on DeepCAD point clouds and 92.2 IoU from multi-view images** ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v1)). Ortho2CAD (Jul 2026) applied the same recipe to orthographic drawings with a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B. It reached IoU 0.792 on DeepCAD and 0.560 on Fusion360, beating zero-shot GPT-5.2 (0.599 and 0.518). It still covers only sketch-and-extrude, and it sees only bounding-box dimensions ([Ortho2CAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08891v1)).

The third lineage, **agentic, verified, typed-IR systems**, dominates 2026, because the 2026 benchmarks showed how narrow the earlier gains were. BenchCAD's 17,900 programs span 106 industrial part families. On it, frontier models recover coarse shape but swap sweeps, lofts and twist-extrudes for simple extrudes, and they misread industrial parameters ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10865)). Text2CAD-Bench puts the cliff at its L3 tier (sweep, loft, shell, freeform), where the best IoU falls to 0.28. Its text and table disagree on L3 invalidity rates, so treat that figure cautiously ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)). Even on real scans, cadrille drops from 90 to about 68 IoU ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v1)).

Verification loops are the most consistent fix. In CADSmith, a planner emits a JSON spec, a coder writes CadQuery, an executor runs it, and an Opus VLM validates 3-view renders together with OCCT metrics. That loop cut mean Chamfer distance on complex parts from **49.7 without vision to 1.4 with it**. On simple parts, vision added almost nothing ([CADSmith](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26512v1)). CADSmith also documents the key failure mode: a quadcopter frame scored IoU 0.985 but had gaps between the arms and the hub that neither the metrics nor the VLM caught. A cheaper option needs no verifier at all: sample N programs and pick the one that agrees most with the others. This cut Chamfer distance by 1–10% compared with random selection ([Consensus TTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09706)).

The architectural trend is a typed IR between the LLM and the kernel, which is what Shilpa already has. CADIR (Aug 2026) is an executable OCCT IR that records operations, parameter dependencies and topology selections in a construction graph. It uses "geometric signature matching" to re-identify faces and edges, so adapters can rebuild native FreeCAD, SolidWorks and Fusion feature trees. That is nearly Shilpa's design, although the abstract gives no numbers ([CADIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00891)). CADDesigner argues that build123d's context managers and operator overloading make it awkward for LLMs to write directly ([CADDesigner](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01031v5)). Shilpa's LLM never writes build123d, so this does not apply.

Assemblies follow the same pattern one level up. ArtiCAD, AssemCAD and Embodied CAD all have an LLM emit a symbolic assembly spec first: parts, geometry-backed ports or connectors, and typed mates. A deterministic solver or mate library then places the geometry. ArtiCAD's ablation is the best direct evidence that interfaces must come first: predicting relationships *after* generating geometry cut success from 100% to 89.2% ([ArtiCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10992v1); [AssemCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05123); [Embodied CAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31252)).

Inputs other than text follow the same split. **Drawings** work best as VLM-to-code for geometry, with hybrid detector + OCR + fine-tuned VLM pipelines for annotations. A fine-tuned Florence-2 beat zero-shot GPT-4o and Claude on annotation F1 by 52.4% ([Khan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03707)). No published work reconstructs mated assemblies from multi-sheet assembly drawings. **Photos** give proposal geometry without absolute scale. **Scans** split into two routes. Code-generating models (CAD-Recode, cadrille) suit prismatic parts. Segment-and-fit B-rep methods (Point2CAD, NVDNet) suit freeform parts but give no feature history ([CAD-Recode](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/67036/1/CADRecode_ICCV_2025.pdf); [Point2CAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.04962)). **Visual meshes** are now fast and good: TRELLIS.2 is MIT-licensed, has 4B parameters and takes about 3 s at 512³ on an H100. A mesh, though, is the opposite of a valid solid: TRELLIS.2 deliberately models non-manifold and internal geometry. Routing images through a mesh into CAD gave a 14% invalid rate and 69.8 IoU, against 0% and 92.2 for direct image-to-CAD ([TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2); [cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v1)).

## Vendors ship single parts, assistants and unmated assemblies

What actually ships in October 2026 is narrower than vendor marketing suggests. **Zoo** has the most complete code-as-CAD stack. It has its own GPU kernel, the KCL language (now v3.0), an open-source Design Studio app and the Zookeeper agent ([Zoo](https://zoo.dev/blog/zoo-design-studio-v1); [ENGtechnica](https://engtechnica.com/kcl-3-0-expands-cad-modeling-while-protecting-existing-designs/)). Its STEP export drops the KCL history, and assembly mates are still a roadmap item. The only independent multi-tool test, by Xometry in August 2025, rated it 2/5. It handled a simple cylinder but got a 24-tooth involute gear wrong and did not generate a 150×100×50 manifold with internal channels at all. Adam did best (4/5): it got the gear right and simplified the manifold, but it exports only STL or OpenSCAD ([Xometry](https://xometry.pro/en/articles/text-to-cad-tools-test/)).

**MecAgent**, the closest analogue to Shilpa, runs an agent inside live SolidWorks. It sells drawing-to-CAD on tight quotas: 1 per month on the $84 tier and 6 per month at $417 per seat ([mecagent.com](https://mecagent.com)). That suggests the feature is expensive to run or still unreliable. **Leo AI** demonstrated "full assemblies from a prompt" (a hydraulic piston) around March 2026, but **the parts are not mated automatically**, and the demo video skips the human steps ([Engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/leo-ai-can-now-generate-full-cad-assemblies/)). **Backflip** went GA around August 2026 with scan or mesh to a *parametric feature tree*, as a Fusion add-in and a web app. That makes a feature tree, not dumb B-rep, the bar for reverse engineering ([VoxelMatters](https://www.voxelmatters.com/backflip-ai-launches-cad-copilot-that-turns-3d-scans-into-editable-models/)).

The incumbents ship assistance. Dassault scheduled ten SolidWorks AI tools for July 2026 GA, including Assembly Structure Designer (an assembly skeleton, not detailed geometry), Drawing Creation, and an xDesign beta that turns a **PDF drawing into a parametric part**. That beta is the most direct threat to Shilpa's drawing-to-CAD pitch, but it is browser- and 3DEXPERIENCE-only. Chat-driven modeling was still "in development" ([Engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/10-ai-tools-coming-to-solidworks-in-2026/)). Autodesk's neural CAD, a prompt-to-editable-B-rep foundation model, was announced at AU 2025 and appears only on the Fusion roadmap. What has shipped is AutoConstrain ([DEVELOP3D](https://develop3d.com/cad/autodesk-shows-its-ai-hand/); [Engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/fusion-autoconstrain-is-now-live-and-it-might-actually-be-useful/)). Siemens ships a help copilot, AI "Magnetic Snap" mating of existing parts, and automated drawings covering "up to 80%" of views ([Siemens](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/solidedge/designcenter-solid-edge-2026-artificial-intelligence/)). Onshape's AI Advisor explicitly does not generate geometry, but PTC's FeatureScript MCP server opens Onshape to external agents ([Leo AI comparison](https://www.getleo.ai/blog/built-in-ai-cad-plm-comparison-2026), a competitor source). Mesh vendors (Meshy, Tripo, Rodin, Hunyuan3D) output GLB, OBJ or STL, never STEP or feature trees ([3DPrinting.com](https://3dprinting.com/software-guides/ai-3d-model-generators/)).

No vendor has shown sheet-metal generation, fully mated assemblies from text, or validated manufacturability. No AI-generated whole product (bicycle, drone or vehicle) with mates and a BOM exists in public. Bicycle AI work stops at BikeCAD parameters. BikeBench found that **LLMs trail hybrid generative-plus-optimisation methods** on constraint satisfaction and quality ([BikeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00830v2)). That leaves a gap Shilpa can occupy: assemblies with real mates and interface checks, sheet metal with flat patterns, FreeCAD feature trees that keep their history, drawing-to-CAD with dimensional cross-checks, and a published, reproducible benchmark. The Xometry test notes the field has none.

## A spec-first hierarchy turns "dots and lines" into products

The current codebase is a sound base but deliberately narrow. `shilpa/contracts/ir.py` defines a `Recipe` (schema `1.0`, `intent: "engineering_cad"`) with a discriminated union of **12 ops** (centered_rectangle, circle, extrude, stepped_shaft, hole, three hole patterns, pocket, boolean, fillet, chamfer), capped at **50 features**. Its `Assertions` cover solid count, B-rep validity, bbox, volume and hole groups. Edges are picked by a heuristic `EdgeSelector` (direction, location, curve, boundary). `Param.source` already records provenance (user, drawing, assumed, derived, default), which is valuable and should spread to every level above. The plan below keeps this recipe as the leaf of a tree and adds layers above it, without replacing it.

### Five layers, each with a typed contract and its own checks

**Layer 1, the product spec**, is a new `contracts/product.py`. It holds requirements (use case, rider height, materials, governing standards), a product class or template id, and a **product tree**: subsystems, then parts, each node tagged make, buy or standard. The research found no AI paper that generates multi-level BOMs or uses skeleton models. Even ArtiCAD decomposes only one level, from product to parts plus connectors ([ArtiCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10992v1)). So this layer comes from established CAD practice rather than research. FreeCAD 1.x's Assembly workbench already produces parametric BOMs and exploded views on TechDraw pages, so the export target exists ([LibreArts](https://librearts.org/2024/11/freecad-1-0/)).

**Layer 2, the skeleton or master layout**, is a parametric set of named points, axes and planes. For a bicycle these are the BB centre, head-tube top and bottom, seat cluster, dropouts and wheel axles, computed from stack, reach, head and seat angles, chainstay length and BB drop. Every part references the skeleton, never another part. This also gets around ArtiCAD's limitation that its kinematic tree cannot contain closed loops. A bike frame is a structural loop, but modelled as one weldment part on a skeleton it needs no loop-closing mates ([ArtiCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10992v1)). Following BikeBench, the LLM should choose topology and initial values, and a numeric optimiser plus rule checker should refine the skeleton.

**Layer 3, interfaces**, is a new `contracts/assembly.py`. It holds **ports** (a named local frame: origin, primary axis, reference axis, semantic label, and a *dimension contract* such as "1-1/8 in steerer", "68 mm BSA shell", "12×142 thru-axle" or "27.2 mm seatpost") and **mates**: `{a.port, b.port, type ∈ fixed|revolute|slider|cylindrical|ball|planar|screw|gear/belt coupler, offsets, limits}`. This is the cross-vendor representation: Onshape mate connectors are frames with x/y/z axes ([Onshape](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/mateconnector.htm)). It maps one-to-one onto build123d's `RigidJoint`, `RevoluteJoint`, `LinearJoint`, `CylindricalJoint` and `BallJoint` for placement, and onto FreeCAD Assembly joints for export ([build123d joints](https://build123d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/joints.html); [Ondsel handbook](https://ondsel.com/handbook/Operation/freecad-contributions)). As ArtiCAD and AssemCAD both show, all ports and mates are fixed **before** any part geometry exists. Each part agent then receives its ports as hard constraints, and the validator checks that the built B-rep actually exposes a face or axis matching each declared port ([AssemCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05123)).

**Layer 4, part-level IR agents**, is the existing planner and recipe pipeline, run once per make-part with the port contracts injected. Repeated or mirrored parts are derived as copies rather than regenerated, as ArtiCAD does. Standard parts never go through an LLM. A catalog node resolves to `{standard, size, generator, predefined ports, supplier part numbers}`. bd_warehouse (Apache-2.0) covers fasteners, flanges and pipes ([bd_warehouse](https://bd-warehouse.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_sources/index.rst.txt)). Bicycle content (headsets, BB cups, hubs, rims to ISO 5775, cassettes, tyres) is in no library, so Wayam must build an "interface-envelope" library keyed to those standards.

**Layer 5, assembly solve and checks**, is a new `shilpa/validators/assembly.py`. Placement is deterministic via build123d joints in a Compound tree; `connect_to()` places once and does not bind, so relationships live in the spec ([build123d joints](https://build123d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/joints.html)). The checks are these. Pairwise **interference** is the volume of an OCCT boolean common, whitelisted for declared press-fits and threads. **Clearance** is BRepExtrema minimum distance against per-interface targets. **Contact-gap** checks verify that every fixed or welded mate actually touches; this is the quadcopter lesson from CADSmith ([CADSmith](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26512v1)). **DOF accounting** sums per-joint DOF (fixed 0, revolute and slider 1, cylindrical 2, ball 3) and compares the total with the intended mechanism. **Swept-motion interference** samples joint ranges, for example steering lock against the down tube, and crank rotation against the chainstay and the rider's heel. **BOM consistency** checks that every tree node has geometry and every standard part resolves. No documented interference tool exists in the FreeCAD Assembly workbench, so these checks must be Shilpa's own OCCT passes. For physics later, export URDF as ArtiCAD does.

When a check fails, a rollback router decides whether the error is in the code (one part's recipe) or in the design (the spec or skeleton) and reruns only the responsible agent. Removing rollback cut ArtiCAD's success from 100% to 95% ([ArtiCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10992v1)).

### The part vocabulary must grow before assemblies are worth building

Assemblies of extrudes will not satisfy users, and BenchCAD shows that models silently downgrade advanced operations ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10865)). Extend the `Feature` union in roughly this order. First, **general constrained sketches**: polyline, arc, spline and slot, with dimensions. Next come **revolve, sweep along path, loft, shell, draft, mirror and general patterns**, then **helix and thread** (cosmetic plus modelled), **ribs and bosses**, and **counterbore, countersink and tapped hole series** keyed to standards.

Two vocabularies deserve their own sub-IR. A **tube/weldment** sub-IR has members `{start_node, end_node, profile (OD, wall, taper or ovalisation), material}`. It builds them as sweeps on skeleton lines, makes mitres as booleans against neighbouring tubes, and outputs cut lists and mitre templates. That is fully deterministic geometry with no AI paper behind it, but it is feasible in OCCT today. A **sheet-metal** sub-IR covers base flange, edge flange, hem, bend relief, louvers and a K-factor table, and it unfolds to DXF. The FreeCAD SheetMetal workbench already unfolds constant-thickness parts made of planar walls and cylindrical bends, with K-factor lookup ([FreeCAD wiki](https://wiki.freecad.org/SheetMetal_Unfold)). Shilpa can either reuse it through its FreeCAD adapter (check its licence first) or build the bends natively in build123d so that the unfold is correct by construction. BenDFM's 20k labelled bending parts can serve as a DFM oracle, since no sheet-metal *generation* benchmark exists ([BenDFM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13102v1)).

Topology references are the hidden prerequisite. Fillets and edits will break the heuristic `EdgeSelector` as feature counts grow. CADEngBench found that edits touching existing history pass only 40–46% of the time, against 247 of 248 for an independent body addition ([CADEngBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1)). Adopt CADIR-style persistent naming: each face and edge is identified by the feature that created it plus a geometric signature, so selections survive parameter changes and can be rebuilt as native FreeCAD references ([CADIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00891)). Raise the 50-feature cap per part, and add a separate cap per assembly.

### Verification becomes test-driven, render-critiqued and sampled

Today's `Assertions` should turn into **per-requirement executable tests**, modelled on CADTestBench's categories: validity, topology, geometric types, dimensions and ratios, volume, and spatial arrangement. The planner should generate the tests from the requirements *before* the geometry exists, and a separate pass should check the tests themselves against mutants. CADTestBench's tests reach a mutation score above 90%. Test-based scoring agreed with human labels at **AUC 0.928, against about 0.66 for Chamfer distance, CLIP or a VLM judge** ([CADTests](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07807v1)). Shilpa's exact-oracle validators are already closer to this than to IoU, which is a real advantage.

On top of the tests, add three things. First, a **render-plus-VLM critique** run only on complex or freeform tiers, where it pays off (CADSmith). It works best as CADCodeVerify-style yes/no questions rather than free-form scoring ([CADCodeVerify](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.05340)). Second, **N-sample consensus** for ambiguous prompts and photo inputs. Third, the existing STEP-reimport validators at every level: part, subassembly and product. RealCADBench found that GPT-5.5 used as an agent *raised* executability and IoU but *lowered* the semantic judge score by about 7 points ([RealCADBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03773v1)). Gate on all three signals together, never on one.

### Two lanes, engineering CAD and visual mesh, never confused

Make `Recipe.intent` a real switch. The **engineering lane** produces typed IR, B-rep, STEP and FCStd, all validated. The **visual lane** produces a concept mesh: TRELLIS.2 under MIT is the default, and a part-aware generator such as PartCrafter or Hunyuan3D-Part can split it into parts. These meshes are used for renders, silhouette scoring of photo inputs, and "make it look like this" styling references, and they are *always* labelled non-engineering ([TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2); [PartCrafter](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05573v1)). The models need Linux and at least 24 GB of GPU memory, so the visual lane should run as an Azure GPU service rather than on the Windows dev box.

Routing by input type works like this. **Dimensioned drawings** stay on the current path. **Multi-sheet or assembly drawings** get sheet and view segmentation, then title-block, BOM-table and balloon parsing, then per-part drawing-to-IR, then BOM-guided assembly; this matches VisCAD's staging ([VisCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03811)). **Photos** become a proposal IR plus N candidates scored by silhouette IoU, plus a mandatory dimension or scale question. **Scans of prismatic parts** get a proposal followed by OCC distance fitting and a deviation heat-map. **Freeform scans** become a segment-and-fit B-rep or a mesh flagged "non-parametric". Every artefact carries the provenance and deviation the current `Param.source` field already begins to capture.

### The staged capability ladder

| Stage | Ships | Key builds in this codebase | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 Rich single parts | Sweeps, lofts, shells, threads, constrained sketches; Xometry gear and manifold pass | Extend `Feature` union; persistent naming; per-requirement tests; N-sample consensus; VLM critique for hard tier | Feasible now |
| S2 Manufacturing families | Sheet metal with flat pattern; tube weldments; standard-part catalog; CAD-to-drawing output | Sheet-metal and weldment sub-IRs; `catalog/` with bd_warehouse plus own standards; TechDraw export | Feasible now (needs effort) |
| S3 Assemblies | 5–30-part mated assemblies with BOM, interference, clearance, DOF | `contracts/assembly.py` (ports/mates); `validators/assembly.py`; build123d joint placement; FreeCAD Assembly export; rollback router | Feasible now; no off-the-shelf code (2026 papers released none) |
| S4 Template products | Bicycle, enclosure-with-PCB, conveyor module end to end | `contracts/product.py`; skeletons per class; optimiser on skeleton params; swept-motion checks; URDF | Feasible with templates; open-ended decomposition is research |
| S5 Multimodal products | Multi-sheet assembly drawings, photo or scan to assembly proposal, visual lane | Drawing segmentation and BOM parser; scan fitting with deviation report; TRELLIS.2 service | Drawings: experimental. Photo and scan: proposal-grade only |
| Research track | Freeform NURBS from text, closed-loop linkages, own fine-tuned model | B-rep generators for styling; RL fine-tune on self-generated data (Ortho2CAD recipe) | Research-stage |

The bicycle request then decomposes concretely. The skeleton comes from a size template. The frame and fork are tube weldments. Wheels, headset, BB, drivetrain and brakes come from the interface-envelope library as buy parts with ports. Cockpit and seatpost are lofted or swept parts. The saddle shell goes to the visual lane, or to a lofted approximation flagged freeform. The checks are steering and crank sweeps, tyre-to-frame clearance, heel clearance and chainline. This design keeps the LLM on the choices it is good at (topology, standards, parameters) and away from generating threads, bearings or free surfaces.

## Licences shut most open models and datasets out of training

Most of the field's best open artefacts are non-commercial. cadrille weights are **CC BY-NC 4.0** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/maksimko123/cadrille-rl)), and so is the CAD-Recode v1.5 dataset ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/filapro/cad-recode-v1.5)). Text2CAD is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SadilKhan/Text2CAD)). The Fusion 360 Gallery, including its assembly data, permits "only non-commercial research" ([Autodesk LICENSE.md](https://github.com/AutodeskAILab/Fusion360GalleryDataset/blob/master/LICENSE.md)). ABC's MIT label defers to the creators' Onshape copyright, which leaves commercial training rights unclear ([NYU](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/44368)). Point2CAD's README says CC-BY-NC even though its sidebar says Apache-2.0 ([GitHub](https://github.com/YujiaLiu76/Point2CAD)). Hunyuan3D's open weights exclude the EU, UK and South Korea and cap use at 1M MAU ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15442)). The FreeCAD Fasteners workbench is GPL-2: fine to call inside a user's FreeCAD, but it should not be vendored into a proprietary backend ([FreeCAD wiki](https://wiki.freecad.org/Fasteners%20Workbench)).

The licence-clean stack is build123d and bd_warehouse (Apache-2.0), TRELLIS.2 (MIT), the Azure OpenAI planner, and **self-generated data**. Shilpa's own generators can produce unlimited (recipe, STEP, drawing, render) tuples, which is how CAD-Recode built its million-sample corpus and Ortho2CAD built its drawings ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v1); [Ortho2CAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08891v1)). Use external benchmarks as evaluation only, after legal review of each data licence. Several were unverified at research time: CADTestBench data, MUSE, OmniMech, RealCADBench and Text2CAD-Bench, and the last two are not yet released. The Ondsel solver's licence (believed LGPL) and its maintenance after Ondsel wound down also need checking before Shilpa depends on it headless.

## A five-level readiness ladder with numeric gates

There is no industry-standard maturity ladder for AI CAD. Cadence's L1–L5 autonomy levels for design agents set no thresholds, and they insist a level is meaningless without scope, validation method, fallback and human decision points ([SemiEngineering](https://semiengineering.com/autonomy-levels-for-design-agents-l1-to-l5-explained/)). So Shilpa should report two axes: a **capability level R1–R5** and an **autonomy level**, where L2 means natural language drives the tools, L3 means self-verifying with engineer review (where Shilpa is today), and L4 means a multi-agent workflow where the engineer sets goals and acceptance criteria. The gates below build on published best scores. They are proposals, not external standards. The common anchor is that the planner, GPT-5.5, already scores about 0.55 on RealCADBench and 52% on MUSE unaided, so Shilpa's harness must add clear value on top of its own base model.

| Level | Scope | External benchmarks and gates | Internal suites and gates |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 Simple parts | Prismatic and turned, CADPrompt-class | CADTestBench Pass Rate ≥0.80 (abstract) / ≥0.65 (detailed), beating the best published 0.81 / 0.625, with invalid rate <2% ([CADTests](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07807v1)); Text2CAD-Bench L1/L2 IoU ≥0.62 / ≥0.50 once released ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)) | Current 60-task eval rescored as requirement pass rate, not build rate; 30 golden parts; all three Xometry prompts verified dimensionally ([Xometry](https://xometry.pro/en/articles/text-to-cad-tools-test/)) |

Four disciplines make these numbers trustworthy. **First, score requirements, not builds.** CADEngBench found that 58% of executable programs violated at least one requirement, so the "97%" headline should be retired in favour of Pass Rate and Requirement Score ([CADEngBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1)). **Second, test the oracles.** Keep a mutant set and require a mutation score of at least 90% before trusting a suite, as CADTestBench does. **Third, calibrate the VLM judges.** MUSE's judges correlate with humans at only r≈0.66–0.71 per criterion, and human annotators themselves agree at only κ=0.54 on text-to-CAD correctness, so judges should be advisory and every product-level release needs a blinded engineer panel ([MUSE](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28579v2); [CADTests](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07807v1)). **Fourth, add field metrics** no paper reports: first-pass engineer acceptance, median edits and minutes to accept, and time saved against manual CAD. Their targets must be set from Wayam's own pilot baselines.

Publishing Shilpa's internal assembly and product suites, built licence-clean from its own generators, would itself be a market contribution. The field has no reproducible commercial benchmark, and RealCADBench and Text2CAD-Bench were unreleased at research time.

## Conclusion

The main finding is that the bottleneck has moved from generation to specification and verification. Raw geometric skill has commoditised: a 2B model with RL nearly solves sketch-and-extrude, and Shilpa's own planner already reaches about 0.55 on industrial benchmarks without help. The 2026 papers that make real progress on assemblies all win by putting a typed spec ahead of the geometry and a deterministic solver behind it, and Shilpa's typed-IR, validate-everything design gives it a head start there. The real risk is building the wrong thing first. A spectacular "bicycle" demo on a 12-op vocabulary with no interference or contact checks would repeat Leo's unmated assemblies and CADSmith's gapped quadcopter. Grow the vocabulary and the test oracles first, then add ports and mates, and treat product templates as the way to make whole products tractable rather than as a shortcut.

Two caveats. Many 2026 results cited here (AssemCAD, Embodied CAD, CADIR, VisCAD) were read only at abstract level and released no code. Shilpa should therefore expect to implement these patterns itself, and should re-baseline the readiness gates when Text2CAD-Bench, RealCADBench and newer models publish full results. Freeform styling surfaces, open-ended decomposition of products with no template, and closed-loop mechanisms remain honest research problems. Shilpa should route them to the visual lane or to an engineer rather than fake them.
